## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. SGSI+ at Banco de Bogotá (automation, impact, compliance)
**S:** Banco de Bogotá's Security and Cybersecurity CoE managed ISO/IEC 27001:2022 audit evidence for its ISMS through a manual, spreadsheet-based process spread across many bank departments.
**T:** As a business intern (and as my degree thesis), I had to design a solution that automated evidence requests, control documentation and tracking for the audit.
**A:** I built SGSI+ on Power Apps, Power Automate, SharePoint and Power BI: 9 automated Power Automate workflows for requesting, following up on and collecting evidence; Power BI dashboards (Power Query, star-schema model) tracking evidence status, findings and closure times; and I gathered requirements with the business areas in agile iterations.
**R:** 1,361 evidence requests processed with a 90.4% delivery rate; the solution was recognized by the external auditor; my university graded the internship "Sobresaliente".
**Use for:** "Tell me about an achievement you're proud of", "Describe a process you improved", "How do you handle compliance/audit work?", "Tell me about a project you owned end to end"

### 2. Hand-labeled ResNet50 classifier (ownership, persistence, learning)
**S:** I wanted a browser extension that could classify photos in Google Photos, but there was no suitable labeled dataset.
**T:** Build the dataset and a model small and fast enough to run in the browser.
**A:** I labeled 17,000+ images by hand, fine-tuned a pretrained ResNet50 with transfer learning in PyTorch on my GPU (CUDA) instead of training from scratch, then exported it to ONNX so it could run in a JavaScript browser extension.
**R:** A working extension running the model on Google Photos (github.com/Pidual/Photo-Deletor-Extension). *[Add any accuracy figure you measured before using it in an interview.]*
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you did tedious work to reach a goal", "How do you learn new technologies?", "Describe a technical challenge"

### 3. EPAM Cloud Platform Engineer Program - AWS (fast learning, cloud security)
**S:** I wanted to move toward cloud security, but my professional experience was in GRC, not AWS.
**T:** Build real hands-on AWS skills while working.
**A:** I completed EPAM's AWS program on the Syndicate platform: all 39 practical labs covering IAM policy evaluation, conditions and role chaining, KMS-encrypted S3, VPC peering and Transit Gateway, Multi-AZ RDS, DMS migration, CloudFormation, CodeBuild/CodeDeploy and CloudWatch/SNS alarms.
**R:** Score 3460/3900 (pass mark 3440), 2nd place on the course leaderboard; certificate expected Dec 2026.
**Use for:** "Why cloud security?", "How do you keep your skills current?", "Tell me about a time you taught yourself something"

### 4. Security homelab (initiative, hands-on security)
**S:** My internship was GRC-focused, and I wanted to see the controls I audited from the technical side.
**T:** Build an environment to practice networking, monitoring and testing.
**A:** I set up Proxmox VE on a Ryzen 7 7800H; deployed OPNsense with VLAN segmentation, port/protocol firewall rules and VPN; ran Windows/Linux VMs with basic Active Directory; centralized logs in the ELK Stack; scanned with Nessus/OpenVAS; tested with Kali, Metasploit, Wireshark and Nmap.
**R:** A working lab I use to validate controls and learn tools. Be honest that it is lab experience, not production.
**Use for:** "What do you do outside work to grow?", SOC-role technical questions

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why are you looking to leave your current role?"
> My role at Profound is an independent-contractor engagement through a staffing firm. I'm looking for a long-term position where I can grow, especially toward cloud security, and contribute beyond compliance documentation.

### "You don't have [specific skill/experience]."
> Acknowledge the gap directly, then bridge: GRC controls knowledge + hands-on AWS labs + homelab, and the track record of learning fast (Security+, EPAM 2nd place, SGSI+ built from scratch).

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> Working as a cloud security engineer or senior GRC/cloud-compliance specialist: designing and automating controls in cloud environments, not just documenting them.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> Some of my technical skills (AWS, SIEM, Active Directory) come from labs rather than production. I mitigate this by building real environments, the homelab and 39 AWS labs, and I'm looking for a role where I can apply them on live systems.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific projects, company values, market position, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the development/project lifecycle look like, from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for [relevant area]?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or strategic decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between development/new projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
